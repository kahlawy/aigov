Doctoral Research Proposal, Version 17

Governance-by-Design Runtime Enforcement and Obligation-to-Evidence Traceability in Agentic DevSecOps

An interactive proof-of-concept that demonstrates the proposed mechanisms and planned evaluation architecture. It does not present completed empirical findings.

PROTOTYPE, NOT STUDY RESULTS

Research problem

Agentic software systems can autonomously invoke repositories, pipelines, and deployment tools, while conventional controls are often fragmented, static, or applied after execution.

Research gap

There is no empirically validated software engineering framework that combines preventive runtime governance with end-to-end obligation-to-evidence traceability for Agentic DevSecOps.

Core scientific contribution

A Governance-by-Design software engineering framework that mediates agent tool actions before execution and produces reconstructable evidence linked to governing obligations.

Primary research question

To what extent does the proposed framework reduce critical false permits while preserving legitimate task completion, evidence quality, and acceptable operational cost?

Independent variable

Governance condition, B1, B2, or F.

Primary outcome

CFPR, Critical False Permit Rate.

Validation

Frozen scenarios, repeated runs, mechanism ablations, supporting measures, and reproducibility manifests.

Prototype boundary

This demonstrator uses synthetic deterministic fixtures, a non-cryptographic demonstration checksum, and simulated tools. It illustrates the artifact, trace model, and planned measurement workflow only. The final study will use implemented conditions, frozen scenarios, cryptographic evidence protection, and empirical statistical analysis.
